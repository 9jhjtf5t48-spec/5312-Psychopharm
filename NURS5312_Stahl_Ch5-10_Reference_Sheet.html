Ch 5

AntipsychoticsTargeting dopamine & serotonin receptors

Network Antipsychotics primarily block D2 receptors across four dopamine pathways: mesolimbic (positive symptoms — the therapeutic target), mesocortical (negative/cognitive), nigrostriatal (movement → EPS), and tuberoinfundibular (prolactin). Atypicals add 5-HT2A antagonism, which raises dopamine in the nigrostriatal/tuberoinfundibular pathways → fewer EPS and lower prolactin than typicals.
ClassMechanism / targetExamples & notes
Typicals (FGA)D2 antagonism (high-potency → EPS; low-potency → H1/α1/M1)High: haloperidol, fluphenazine · Low: chlorpromazine, thioridazine"HAL blocks FLUently" (EPS); "CHLOR makes you lazy" (sedation/orthostasis). High prolactin. Thioridazine: notable QT prolongation.
Atypicals (SGA)D2 antagonism + 5-HT2A antagonismrisperidone, paliperidone, olanzapine, quetiapine, ziprasidone, lurasidoneFewer EPS than typicals; metabolic risk. Risperidone & paliperidone = highest prolactin. Ziprasidone (≥500 cal) & lurasidone (≥350 cal) need food.
Partial agonistsD2 (± D3) partial agonism — "dopamine stabilizers"aripiprazole, brexpiprazole, cariprazineLow prolactin, weight-neutral. Brexpiprazole = less akathisia; cariprazine (D3) targets negative/cognitive symptoms.
ClozapineBroad D2/5-HT2A + multi-receptor; reserved for treatment resistanceclozapineUniquely reduces suicidality. Agranulocytosis (ANC/REMS), myocarditis (wks 4–6), seizures, sialorrhea (M1/M4); highest metabolic risk. Not first-line.
Stahl / Vela concepts
  • D2 occupancy: ~60–80% needed for efficacy; exceeding ~80% → EPS and hyperprolactinemia.
  • Cardiometabolic tiers: High = clozapine, olanzapine · Moderate = risperidone, paliperidone · Low = aripiprazole, ziprasidone, lurasidone.
  • "Serotonin breaks the brake": 5-HT2A antagonism raises nigrostriatal dopamine → fewer EPS, and lowers prolactin (serotonin normally raises prolactin).
  • EPS treatments: akathisia → propranolol; dystonia → benztropine/diphenhydramine; parkinsonism → amantadine/anticholinergic; tardive dyskinesia → VMAT2 inhibitor (avoid anticholinergics); NMS → stop drug + dantrolene/bromocriptine.
Ch 7

Treatments for Mood Disorders"Antidepressants" and "mood stabilizers"

Network Antidepressants act on the trimonoamine system — serotonin (5-HT), norepinephrine (NE), and dopamine (DA) — mostly by blocking reuptake transporters (SERT, NET, DAT) or blocking specific receptors. Benefit is delayed (≈2–6 wks) because it depends on downstream receptor desensitization and neuroplasticity (BDNF), not the immediate transporter block.
ClassMechanism / targetExamples & notes
SSRIsBlock serotonin reuptake (SERT)fluoxetine, sertraline, paroxetine, citalopram, escitalopram, fluvoxamineFirst-line; GI/sexual SE, hyponatremia, discontinuation syndrome.
SNRIsBlock SERT + NET (serotonin & norepinephrine)venlafaxine, desvenlafaxine, duloxetine, levomilnacipranAlso used in pain; dose-dependent BP rise.
NDRIBlock NET + DAT (norepinephrine & dopamine; no serotonin)bupropionActivating; weight-neutral; spares sexual function; lowers seizure threshold.
α2-antagonist (NaSSA)Blocks α2 (disinhibits NE & 5-HT release) + 5-HT2A/2C, 5-HT3, H1mirtazapineSedating, increases appetite; good for insomnia/low weight; minimal sexual SE.
SARIs5-HT2A antagonist + SERT inhibition (+ H1/α1)trazodone, nefazodoneTrazodone used low-dose for sleep; priapism risk.
MultimodalSERT inhibition + 5-HT1A partial agonism + other 5-HT receptor actionsvortioxetine, vilazodoneVortioxetine targets cognition; lower sexual SE.
TCAsBlock SERT/NET + unwanted H1, M1, α1, and Na⁺-channel blockadeamitriptyline, nortriptyline, imipramineEffective but anticholinergic, orthostatic, cardiotoxic in overdose.
MAOIsInhibit MAO-A/B → raise all monoaminesphenelzine, tranylcypromine, selegilineTyramine diet (hypertensive crisis); washout to avoid serotonin syndrome.
Mood stabilizers"From above" (antimanic) vs "from below" (antidepressant pole)lithium, valproate, carbamazepine (above); lamotrigine (below); atypical antipsychotics (both)Lithium: narrow index, monitor renal/thyroid, ↓suicidality. Lamotrigine: slow titrate (SJS).
Stahl concepts
  • "From above / from below": mania is stabilized from above baseline; bipolar depression from below — lamotrigine works "from below."
  • Delayed onset: transporter block is immediate, but symptom relief waits on receptor down-regulation and neuroplasticity/BDNF.
  • Augmentation (partial response): add lithium, an atypical antipsychotic, L-methylfolate, thyroid (T3), or esketamine.
  • Don't treat bipolar depression with an antidepressant alone — manic-switch risk; pair with a stabilizer/atypical.
Ch 8

Anxiety, Trauma, and TreatmentFear circuits and their pharmacology

Circuit Anxiety/fear is driven by an over-reactive amygdala and its loops with the prefrontal cortex and hippocampus. Treatments either boost serotonin/NE (SSRIs/SNRIs), enhance GABA inhibition (benzodiazepines), or dampen excitatory release via calcium channels (α2δ ligands).
ClassMechanism / targetExamples & notes
SSRIs / SNRIsBlock SERT (± NET)sertraline, escitalopram, paroxetine; venlafaxine, duloxetineFirst-line, durable; delayed onset.
BenzodiazepinesPositive allosteric modulators at GABA-A (↑ Cl⁻ inhibition)lorazepam, clonazepam, alprazolam, diazepamRapid relief; tolerance/dependence — short-term/bridge use only.
5-HT1A partial agonistPartial agonist at 5-HT1AbuspironeGAD; non-sedating, no dependence, delayed onset.
α2δ ligandsBind α2δ subunit of voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels → ↓ excitatory releasepregabalin, gabapentinAlso used in pain; sedation, dizziness.
AntihistamineH1 antagonisthydroxyzineNon-dependence-forming PRN option.
β-blockersBlock peripheral β-adrenergic somatic symptomspropranololPerformance anxiety (tremor, palpitations).
α1 antagonistBlocks α1-adrenergic (reduces noradrenergic nightmares)prazosinPTSD nightmares / sleep disturbance.
Stahl concepts
  • Fear extinction & reconsolidation: CBT/exposure rewires the amygdala-PFC circuit; benzodiazepines can impair extinction learning.
  • Two levers on the same circuit: serotonin/NE (slow, durable) vs GABA (fast, dependence-prone) vs calcium-channel dampening.
  • PTSD: SSRIs (sertraline, paroxetine) first-line; avoid benzodiazepines; prazosin for nightmares.
Ch 9

Chronic Pain and Its TreatmentAscending pain & descending modulation

Pathway Pain signals ascend the spinal cord, while descending NE & 5-HT pathways from the brainstem normally inhibit them. In chronic/neuropathic pain and fibromyalgia, this becomes central sensitization. The drug targets overlap with mood/anxiety — boosting NE/5-HT or dampening calcium-channel excitability.
ClassMechanism / targetExamples & notes
SNRIsBlock SERT + NET → strengthen descending inhibitionduloxetine, milnacipran, levomilnacipranDiabetic neuropathy, fibromyalgia, chronic musculoskeletal pain.
α2δ ligandsBind α2δ of voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels → ↓ excitatory transmissionpregabalin, gabapentinNeuropathic pain, fibromyalgia, postherpetic neuralgia.
TCAsBlock SERT/NET + Na⁺ channelsamitriptyline, nortriptylineNeuropathic pain; anticholinergic limits.
Opioidsμ-opioid receptor agonistsmorphine, oxycodone, hydromorphoneEffective but tolerance, dependence, OUD, respiratory depression.
Na⁺-channel agentsBlock voltage-gated sodium channelscarbamazepine (trigeminal neuralgia), lidocaineSelect neuropathic syndromes.
Stahl concepts
  • Shared circuitry: chronic pain uses the same NE/5-HT and calcium-channel mechanisms as depression/anxiety — which is why antidepressants and anticonvulsants treat pain.
  • Fibromyalgia = central pain: a disorder of central sensitization, not peripheral tissue damage; responds to SNRIs and α2δ ligands, not opioids.
  • Descending inhibition is the therapeutic target SNRIs strengthen.
Ch 10

Sleep and WakefulnessThe wake/sleep switch and its drugs

Switch Wakefulness is driven by the ascending arousal system — histamine (H1), orexin/hypocretin, norepinephrine, dopamine, serotonin, and acetylcholine. Sleep is promoted by GABA (the VLPO "off-switch") and melatonin (circadian). Orexin stabilizes wakefulness — its loss causes narcolepsy. (This is the basis for the Exam-2 fact that sleep is regulated by the RAS/H1/5-HT, not a dopamine pathway.)
ClassMechanism / targetExamples & notes
GABA-A PAMsEnhance GABA-A inhibition (promote sleep)Z-drugs zolpidem, zaleplon, eszopiclone; benzodiazepinesDependence, falls in elderly, complex sleep behaviors.
Melatonin agonistsMT1/MT2 receptor agonism (circadian)ramelteon, melatoninSleep-onset; no dependence.
Orexin antagonists (DORAs)Block orexin receptors → reduce wake drivesuvorexant, lemborexant, daridorexantNewer; for sleep-maintenance insomnia.
H1 antagonistsBlock histamine H1 (promote sleep)low-dose doxepin, diphenhydramineDoxepin selective at low dose; anticholinergic load with diphenhydramine.
SARI (off-label)H1 + 5-HT2A + α1 antagonismtrazodoneCommon off-label sleep aid.
Wake-promoting↑ DA/NE or histamine (treat hypersomnia/narcolepsy)modafinil/armodafinil (DAT), solriamfetol (DNRI), pitolisant (H3 inverse agonist → ↑histamine), stimulants, sodium oxybate (narcolepsy/cataplexy); caffeine (adenosine antagonist)Match to narcolepsy vs residual sleepiness.
Stahl concepts
  • Wake/sleep is a switch: arousal transmitters (H1, orexin, NE, DA, 5-HT, ACh) vs sleep promoters (GABA, melatonin) flip the state.
  • Orexin stabilizes wakefulness; loss of orexin neurons → narcolepsy with cataplexy. Blocking orexin (DORAs) promotes sleep.
  • H1 = the sedation lever: blocking histamine causes sleepiness — the same reason sedating antidepressants and antihistamines make you drowsy.
  • CBT-I is first-line for chronic insomnia; avoid chronic benzodiazepines/Z-drugs.
