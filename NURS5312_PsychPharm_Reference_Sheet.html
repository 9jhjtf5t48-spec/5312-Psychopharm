What treats what — drugs by disorder & combination strategies

A study map of first-line agents, alternatives, and the augmentation/combination strategies used when a single drug isn't enough. Organized by disorder; cross-cutting combinations are grouped at the bottom.

How to read each card: First-line = typical starting choice · Other options = alternatives / later-line / situational · Notes = key cautions and monitoring. Drug names in bold are representative, not exhaustive. Doses are intentionally omitted — this is an indication map, not a prescribing guide.

Major Depressive Disorder

Mood
First-lineSSRIs (sertraline, escitalopram, fluoxetine, citalopram, paroxetine) · SNRIs (venlafaxine, duloxetine, desvenlafaxine)
Other optionsBupropion (NDRI), mirtazapine, vortioxetine, vilazodone; TCAs & MAOIs (later-line); esketamine (treatment-resistant)
4–6 wks for full effect. Watch SIADH/hyponatremia, serotonin syndrome, discontinuation syndrome. Bupropion & mirtazapine spare sexual function; bupropion lowers seizure threshold.

Anxiety Disorders

GAD · Panic · Social
First-lineSSRIs and SNRIs
Other optionsBuspirone (GAD), hydroxyzine, pregabalin/gabapentin; benzodiazepines (short-term bridge only); beta-blockers (performance anxiety)
Avoid long-term benzodiazepines (dependence). Buspirone is non-sedating with no dependence but takes weeks. SSRIs/SNRIs are the durable choice.

Bipolar Disorder

Mood stabilizing
ManiaLithium, valproate, atypical antipsychotics (quetiapine, olanzapine, risperidone, aripiprazole, cariprazine, asenapine)
Bipolar depressionLurasidone, quetiapine, cariprazine, lumateperone; lamotrigine (maintenance); olanzapine-fluoxetine combo
Avoid antidepressant monotherapy (manic-switch risk). Lithium: narrow therapeutic index — monitor levels, renal, thyroid. Lamotrigine: SJS risk, titrate slowly. Valproate: hepatotoxic, teratogenic.

Schizophrenia / Psychosis

Antipsychotics
First-lineAtypicals / SGAs (risperidone, paliperidone, olanzapine, quetiapine, ziprasidone, lurasidone, aripiprazole)
Other optionsTypicals / FGAs (haloperidol, fluphenazine, chlorpromazine); clozapine for treatment-resistant disease
All block D2. Watch EPS, metabolic syndrome, hyperprolactinemia, QT. Clozapine: reserved for resistance, reduces suicidality, requires ANC monitoring (REMS), myocarditis risk weeks 4–6.

OCD

Obsessive-Compulsive
First-lineSSRIs — often at higher doses than for depression
Other optionsClomipramine (TCA); atypical antipsychotic augmentation for refractory cases
Needs higher doses and longer trials (8–12 wks) than depression. Pair with CBT / exposure-response prevention (ERP).

PTSD

Trauma
First-lineSSRIs — sertraline & paroxetine are FDA-approved; SNRIs (venlafaxine)
Other optionsPrazosin (trauma nightmares / sleep); atypical antipsychotic adjunct in select cases
Trauma-focused psychotherapy is first-line overall. Avoid benzodiazepines (ineffective, risky).

ADHD

Attention
First-lineStimulants — methylphenidate & amphetamines (lisdexamfetamine, mixed amphetamine salts)
Other optionsAtomoxetine (NRI), alpha-2 agonists (guanfacine ER, clonidine ER), bupropion (off-label)
Stimulants are controlled substances. Monitor cardiovascular status, growth, sleep, appetite. Non-stimulants useful with tics, anxiety, or misuse risk.

Insomnia

Sleep
First-lineCBT-I (behavioral) is first-line overall
Other optionsTrazodone, melatonin/ramelteon, Z-drugs (zolpidem, eszopiclone), orexin antagonists (suvorexant), low-dose doxepin; mirtazapine if comorbid depression
Avoid chronic benzodiazepines/Z-drugs (dependence, falls in elderly). Match agent to sleep-onset vs sleep-maintenance problem.

Substance Use Disorders

Addiction
AlcoholNaltrexone, acamprosate, disulfiram
Opioid (MOUD)Buprenorphine, methadone, naltrexone
TobaccoVarenicline, bupropion, nicotine replacement
Pair with psychosocial treatment. Naltrexone is contraindicated with current opioid use (precipitates withdrawal). Offer naloxone for OUD.

Alzheimer / Neurocognitive

Dementia
Mild–moderateCholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine)
Moderate–severeMemantine (NMDA antagonist), often added to a cholinesterase inhibitor
Benefit is modest/symptomatic. Avoid anticholinergics (worsen cognition). Newer anti-amyloid antibodies (e.g., lecanemab) are emerging, specialist-managed.

Combos Combination & augmentation strategies

Used when a single agent gives partial response or to target different symptom clusters. "Augmentation" = adding a second agent to boost the first; "combination" = two antidepressants/agents together.

Depression — partial / inadequate response

SSRI/SNRI + bupropion → adds dopamine/NE drive; helps energy, motivation, and counters sexual side effects
SSRI/SNRI + mirtazapine → dual action; helps sleep, appetite, anxiety
Venlafaxine + mirtazapine → "California rocket fuel" (Stahl) — robust dual mechanism
SSRI/SNRI + atypical antipsychotic → aripiprazole, brexpiprazole, quetiapine XR, cariprazine are FDA-approved adjuncts
Antidepressant + lithium or T3 (thyroid) → classic augmentation strategies
Antidepressant + esketamine → treatment-resistant depression (REMS)

Bipolar depression

Lithium or lamotrigine + atypical antipsychotic → mood stabilizer plus an SGA with bipolar-depression evidence
Olanzapine + fluoxetine → fixed combo (Symbyax) for bipolar depression & treatment-resistant depression
Avoid → antidepressant alone (risk of switch to mania)

Schizophrenia

Two failed antipsychotics → switch to clozapine (treatment-resistant standard)
Clozapine + augmentation → reserved/specialist; routine antipsychotic polypharmacy is generally discouraged

Anxiety & ADHD

SSRI/SNRI + short-term benzodiazepine → bridge during the weeks the SSRI takes to work, then taper the benzo
Stimulant + alpha-2 agonist (guanfacine/clonidine) → ADHD augmentation; helps residual symptoms, tics, sleep

High-yield pearls

  • One class, many disorders: SSRIs are first-line for depression, anxiety, OCD, and PTSD.
  • Side-effect-driven picks: bupropion & mirtazapine spare (or improve) sexual function; bupropion is also weight-neutral/activating.
  • Bipolar maintenance anchors: lithium and lamotrigine; lamotrigine favors the depressive pole, not acute mania.
  • Clozapine = resistance: only antipsychotic shown to reduce suicidality; ANC monitoring via REMS.
  • OCD differs: higher SSRI doses, longer trials, ERP therapy.
  • Don't treat bipolar depression with an antidepressant alone — pair with a stabilizer/SGA.
  • Augment, don't pile on: for partial MDD response, add bupropion, mirtazapine, an SGA, or lithium.
  • MOUD & alcohol: buprenorphine/methadone/naltrexone for opioids; naltrexone/acamprosate/disulfiram for alcohol.
